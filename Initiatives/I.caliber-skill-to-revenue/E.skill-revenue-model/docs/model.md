# How skill connects to deals and revenue

Working sample for Caliber. Cody Duke. September 2026.

## TLDR

There is a strong correlation between skill and revenue. That is what to show: a live scored call, the deal it sits on, what that deal did, and what it is worth. Skill may not directly cause the money, and there may not be a scientific causation we can prove, so I kept the language light, the way the prototype does: if this score goes up about this many points, we have seen about this much more close. Caliber owns that math. The product should present it, not invent it.

## The job

Ellis opens Skill Intelligence with one hour before a team meeting or a one-on-one. He can already see Tara is Developing at Value Based Discovery. That does not tell him what to coach.

> "Is she booking the wrong companies, or can she not run discovery?"

That is Ellis, from Voice of the Customer, on Tara. He said he does not know what to tell her. A score by itself does not answer it. A dollar column by itself does not either. Money still belongs next to the skill, so he can see how much sits behind a weak call, and which skill is worth the hour.

## What we can say


| We can say                                                                                                  | We should not say                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| These open deals stalled after a below-Proficient call on this skill. They are worth $X.                    | This score caused those stalls.                   |
| Across customers, moving this score about N points has gone with about $Y more of a book like this closing. | This plan will produce $Y for Tara.               |
| After a dated focus, her live score and these watched deals moved.                                          | Because of the focus.                             |
| Most of her stall sits behind weak calls, so this looks more like skill than the book.                      | A dollar number, by itself, split fit from skill. |


On screen I used “we have seen” and “if this score moves,” not “this will produce.” Claiming proof is how you lose the user. They already name correlation versus causation themselves.

The prototype at `/proto/caliber-skill-on-deals` is this model on a manager surface. The forecast numbers are placeholders so the idea can be reviewed. Caliber replaces them.
