Hi Christina,

Thanks for sharing your portfolio. I went through the site and your case studies. There is a lot of good work here, and a few focused changes would make it easier to see. I know how hard this is to pull together during a search.

01What’s working

  • The work is easy to find. The projects are right there, and Home, About, and Resume stay out of the way.
  • The Schwab case shows how you think. The goals, findings, competitive analysis, sketches, and opportunities make the decisions visible.
  • The projects have range. A login, a chatbot, Apple Business Chat, and Alexa are different kinds of interaction, held together by the same section treatment.
  • The experience strip helps. At the very bottom of the homepage, the companies, roles, and dates give a fast picture of where you have been.

02Worth fixing first

  • The contact link still goes to a template address, jane@portfolio.com, with the subject “You’ve got mail!”
  • Resume opens a page called Styleguide. The file is a 2020 image, so the text cannot be selected, and a phone number is public. A current PDF would be easier to share and to keep up to date.
  • Two titles are swapped. “Apple Business Chat” opens the Alexa case, and “Wells Fargo + Amazon Alexa” opens Apple Chat. The pictures go to the right places.
  • The tab and footer still use the template. The homepage tab says “Portfolio - Webflow HTML website template,” the cases are Project 1 through Project 4, and the footer says © 2020 and links to Webflow.
  • Two images need a cleanup. One Schwab phone is an Imgur embed, with the mark and view count still in the frame. On the chatbot case, a row of phones runs off the right edge.

03Bring the work forward

  • Give the final screens more room. Older home-button frames, and graphics that have been enlarged, make the work feel older than it is. Use a current phone frame, export the diagrams larger, and leave out images that are too small to read. A large screen is worth the scroll.
  • Lead with the problem, then the solution. In the Schwab case, the before-and-after and the InVision prototype are the clearest part, and they sit far down the page. Move those up, and let the research explain how you got there.
  • Say what made each project different. Three of them include a balance, transactions, or an ATM. The interesting part is the constraint: legal copy and screen-reader behavior in the chatbot, designing inside iMessage, and Alexa’s failure lines, such as a bad passcode or an account that is not eligible.
  • Close the loop on the outcome. Schwab already mentions 10.77 million clients, a call spike, and a later test where 6 of 7 people found the new login clear. Add whether it shipped, or what you would watch. “Fewer calls about this step” is enough.

04Homepage and About

  • Make the hello more specific. “A passion for engaging, innovative, and delightful products” is friendly. One sentence about the problems you like, or how you like to work with a team, will stay with someone longer.
  • Use one title. The site currently says UI/UX designer, UX Designer, Product Designer, Senior UI/UX Designer, and User Experience Designer.
  • The 10+ years line is on the homepage as well, at the very bottom, under “My experience.” Keep the years, and the roles just under them. The rest of that sentence can say more than a familiarity with UX standards.
  • Let About say a little more. The photo, and Mochi and Hiro, are worth keeping. A few sentences on how you work would help, and it can stay short. Your resume also shows medical-device and agency work. Mentioning that range would make these four bank cases feel like a selection.
  • Add a year, and a problem line, to each card. Use the same title on the card and in the case. A domain of your own is a nice last step. Check the renewal price before you buy.

I would keep this site. Start with the links, titles, and footer, then move the Schwab solution up and give the final screens more room. I hope this is useful, and I would be glad to look again.

Cody
